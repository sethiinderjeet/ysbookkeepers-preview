FAQ

Questions from Counsellors & Therapists

Tax information last reviewed: October 2026

Do counsellors charge GST in BC?
Since June 20, 2024, qualifying counselling therapy and psychotherapy services have been GST-exempt. Psychologists’ services are also exempt. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, the nature of your services and their healthcare purpose. Coaching, workshops and other services may still be taxable. If you provide only exempt services, you may be able to close your GST account after reviewing your obligations.
Should I incorporate my counselling practice in BC?
It depends on how much you earn, how much you need to take home, your future plans and the cost of running a corporation. We help you run the numbers and decide whether incorporation makes sense. Our free Incorporation Guide is a good place to start.
What business expenses can counsellors and therapists claim?
Eligible expenses may include professional dues, liability insurance, clinical supervision, continuing education, office rent and practice software. Home-office expenses may also qualify if you meet CRA requirements. We help you identify deductions relevant to your practice.
How should I pay myself from my counselling corporation?
You can pay yourself through salary, dividends or a combination of both. The right approach depends on your income needs, taxes, CPP and long-term plans. We help you compare your options.
Should therapists in my group practice be employees or contractors?
It depends on how your practice operates, not just what your agreements say. Factors include control over schedules, independence, equipment and financial risk. We help you understand the tax and payroll implications.
Do self-employed counsellors need to pay CRA tax instalments?
You may need to pay instalments if your net tax owing exceeds $3,000 in the current year and in either of the previous two years. We help you understand your payment obligations and plan ahead, especially when your income changes throughout the year.
